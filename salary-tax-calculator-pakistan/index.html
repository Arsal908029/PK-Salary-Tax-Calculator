How salary tax is calculated in Pakistan

Pakistan taxes salary on a progressive slab system. Your monthly salary is multiplied by 12 to get annual taxable salary. Each slab has a fixed amount of tax plus a percentage on the part of your salary that falls above the slab's starting point. Your monthly tax is then one twelfth of the annual figure. Worked example

Take a monthly salary of Rs. 200,000. Annual salary is Rs. 2,400,000, which falls in the Rs. 2,200,001 to Rs. 3,200,000 slab. The tax is Rs. 116,000 plus 20% of the part above Rs. 2,200,000: 20% of Rs. 200,000 is Rs. 40,000, so annual tax is Rs. 156,000. Divided by 12, monthly tax is Rs. 13,000 and take-home is Rs. 187,000.

Frequently asked questions

Who counts as a salaried individual?

A person whose salary is at least 75% of their total taxable income is treated as salaried and uses the salaried slabs. Otherwise the non-salaried slabs apply.

What is the 75% rule?

If salary is 75% or more of total taxable income, the salaried slabs apply. If you also earn a lot from business, rent or other sources, the non-salaried slabs may apply instead. This calculator does not check this rule.

How is tax deducted from my salary every month?

Your employer estimates your annual tax from your expected taxable salary and deducts roughly one twelfth of it from each monthly salary. This calculator shows the same one-twelfth figure.

What about allowances and deductions?

Open Tax-free allowances in the calculator and enter the part of your monthly salary that is not taxed. The calculator then taxes only the rest. Credits and other deductions are not included.

How do I find the salary that gives me a target take-home?

Switch the calculator to Take-home to salary, enter the amount you want to receive after tax, and it works backwards to the gross salary you would need.

When do the slabs change?

Slabs are normally set by the Finance Act passed around June each year and apply from 1 July. This page shows the rates for tax year 2026-27.

What this calculator does not cover

  • The 75% test. It assumes you qualify for the salaried slabs.
  • Tax credits, such as those for donations, pension contributions or education fees.
  • Other income, such as business, rent, profit on debt or capital gains.
